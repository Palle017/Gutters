FREE HOMEOWNER EDUCATION

Start with what
you can observe.

Gutters can help manage roof runoff. They do not solve every water problem. Explore useful observations and when another assessment may be needed.

What are you noticing?

Open any topic and compare as many as you like. This is general education, not a diagnosis or a recommendation to buy.

Observe only from a dry, safe place at ground level, or from indoors. Do not climb, open a gutter or pipe, enter standing water, or go outside in hazardous weather.

These observations cannot establish a cause, a repair scope, or whether gutters are needed. An appropriate on-site inspection may be needed.

Wet ground between nearby homes

Observe safely

From a safe location, note where water seems to arrive, whether it follows rain, and where existing downspouts end. If you cannot see a source, say so.

When gutter work may help

If roof runoff contributes, gutters and an appropriate discharge route may help manage that part of the water.

What this does not establish

Wet ground alone does not establish roof runoff as the cause. Gutters will not correct site grading, irrigation or other water sources.

A useful next step

For persistent or unclear wet areas, ask an appropriate drainage or landscape professional to assess the source and route. John offers underground drainage, but does not offer grading. A site assessment comes before a scope of work.

Water spilling over an existing gutter

Observe safely

If visible safely from indoors or ground level, note whether water spills at one point or along a run, and whether you can see a loose or damaged section. You do not need to look inside the gutter.

When gutter work may help

An inspection can establish whether a cleanout or a localized repair is appropriate before considering replacement.

What this does not establish

Overflow alone does not prove that a new gutter system or leaf guards are needed. The condition, layout and discharge route need assessment.

A useful next step

Ask a gutter professional to inspect the existing system and explain what can be maintained or repaired. Avoid buying a replacement from a symptom alone.

Water collecting near a downspout outlet

Observe safely

From a dry place, note where the outlet ends and where water appears to travel. Leave unknown or concealed pipe routes unknown.

When gutter work may help

An appropriate discharge arrangement may help move collected roof water to a suitable location after a property assessment.

What this does not establish

Adding gutters without considering the outlet may leave the discharge problem unresolved. Underground piping is not a universal solution to standing water.

A useful next step

Have the water source and a suitable discharge location assessed. Do not redirect water toward a neighbor or assume a buried pipe has a usable outlet.

Water indoors, or a wall, window, door or crack concern

Observe safely

Keep a note of the location and timing from a safe place. Do not test a suspected leak or investigate concealed building parts.

When gutter work may help

A gutter inspection may be one part of a professional assessment if roof runoff is relevant.

What this does not establish

This guide cannot diagnose water intrusion, movement, cracks, or wall, roof, window and door conditions. Gutters are not a diagnosis or a promised repair for these concerns.

A useful next step

Arrange an inspection by a qualified professional appropriate to the building concern. Do not delay that assessment while shopping for gutters.

The water source is unclear

Observe safely

If it is safe, note whether water appears after rain, in dry weather, or at times you are unsure about. An existing photo is optional; no measurements are required.

When gutter work may help

Gutters may be relevant if roof runoff is established as a contributor.

What this does not establish

An uncertain source is a reason to investigate, not a reason to recommend gutters. Irrigation, site drainage or another repair may need attention.

A useful next step

Ask an appropriate professional to establish the source first. John can discuss whether the roof-water portion fits his gutter or underground-drainage services; he does not offer grading.
