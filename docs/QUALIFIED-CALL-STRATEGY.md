## First three educational campaigns / briefs
Publish in this order after factual review. These are topic/query hypotheses; no search-volume estimates were obtained.

### 1. Wet ground between homes: follow the water before choosing work
**Input:** recovered interview topic; proposed wording. **Audience/intent:** homeowners wondering whether wet ground means they need gutters.
**Free lesson:** collecting roof water is only one part of a route. A downspout needs a suitable discharge location. Wet ground may require other assessment; John does not grade.
**Outline:** safe observations of source and timing -> what roof-water collection can address -> what it cannot establish -> appropriate site assessment.
**Asset:** a short, captioned explanation using an actual downspout photo; draw a simple conceptual route, clearly an illustration rather than a surveyed design. No customer location or invented success story.
**CTA:** Read the wet-ground card; optionally call/text John with city and what you noticed.
**Qualified outcome:** a received inquiry about a property John can serve where assessing roof-water work is appropriate, later confirmed as a suitable scope. A need for grading alone routes elsewhere.

### 2. Gutter overflow: inspect before buying replacement
**Input:** proposed topic, not recovered John wording. **Audience/intent:** owners seeing water spill from an existing gutter.
**Free lesson:** an observation does not tell you to replace the system. Ask what can be cleaned, maintained or locally repaired and whether the discharge route works.
**Outline:** where/when it spills, if safely observable -> condition/layout questions for an inspection -> maintenance/repair/replacement decisions after inspection -> when cause remains unclear.
**Asset:** factual workmanship-detail photo plus a 30–60 second explanation John actually supplies; no staged dangerous rain footage or gutter-opening instructions.
**CTA:** Read the overflow card; optional call/text about the existing system. No automatic guard upsell.
**Qualified outcome:** suitable cleanout/repair assessment or documented replacement need, not larger quoted footage.

### 3. A downspout is only part of the route
**Input:** proposed topic grounded in general runoff guidance. **Audience/intent:** homeowners seeing water near an outlet or considering underground drainage.
**Free lesson:** more gutter length does not by itself solve where collected water goes. Buried piping needs an assessed route/outlet; standing water can have other sources.
**Outline:** note visible outlet and timing without entering water -> compare collection versus discharge -> limits of underground drainage -> appropriate property assessment, including other trades when necessary.
**Asset:** actual outlet detail and a clearly labeled conceptual diagram; do not present it as approval for a discharge design or give a universal spacing/code rule.
**CTA:** Read the outlet card; if useful, discuss roof runoff/drainage with John.
**Qualified outcome:** a property-specific drainage assessment in scope, not a promise of a dry yard.

Use [DOE runoff/discharge guidance](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/stormwater-gutters-downspouts) for general education and [PNNL site-drainage guidance](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/swales-drains-and-site-grading-stormwater-control) to distinguish other site work. These are general sources, not local design approval. Do not turn them into mold/structural diagnoses, guaranteed savings, or proof that gutters are always required. Indoor water or building concerns warrant an appropriate qualified inspection.

## Qualification and landing experience
First contact can be **name, callback number and short description**. City/ZIP helps confirm coverage; full address, photos and measurements may come later. Do not make a long questionnaire the price of receiving advice.

During a conversation ask:
1. Which city is the property in, and what would you like help with?
2. Where and when do you notice the water? After rain, also in dry weather, or unsure?
3. Are gutters already there? Is this a repair/cleanout question or a possible new installation?
4. Where does the visible downspout end, if known? Has anyone assessed the source?
5. Is there an indoor/building concern that needs a different qualified inspection?
6. Are you the owner or authorized to arrange the work, and what timing do you have in mind?
7. What is the best callback time? An existing ground-level photo is optional; no climbing or measurements needed.

Unknown answers are acceptable. These questions support scope and routing, not a remote diagnosis or a job-value score. Confirm fit after assessment before selling work.

**Guide CTA:** “Read the homeowner guide.” **Contact CTA:** “Call John: (239) 355-3686” / “Text John.” Secondary wording: “Tell John what you noticed and your city.” Education should stay above promotional offers, without countdowns, alarming claims, gated downloads or a contact popup. Keep direct call/text/email fallback until the separate private request service is actually connected and verified. Do not advertise uploads, automatic pricing or instant estimates as working public features.

Match any future paid landing page to the actual service intent: repair/cleanout, installation, or drainage assessment. Keep an optional education link visible. A suitable job can be a small repair; do not set invented minimums. Keep actual workmanship detail captions factual; John must confirm any fastening-method or warranty claims before they are added.

**Phase 1: one bounded Search test.** Propose a two-week observation window, extend only by agreement if counts are too small. Before activation John/Raj specify a total authorized cap, account budget settings, daily exposure, maximum affordable cost per suitable booking, review/stop thresholds, dates and a responsible monitor. Start with one service/area John chooses and a small set of phrase/exact intent hypotheses such as “gutter repair Fort Myers” or “gutter installation Fort Myers”; use “gutter cleaning” only if John wants that work in the test. Do not buy broad “wet yard” or “foundation crack” demand. Use presence-focused location settings, verify geography in received calls and account reports, and confirm the property's location even when a homeowner calls from elsewhere. Location signals are imperfect. [Google location options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038?hl=en)

Use responsive search ads and call assets during confirmed answer coverage. Example copy for review: headline “Gutters or Another Issue?”; headline “Talk With John Wisdom”; description “Read safe observations, then call or text John about gutter work that fits your property.” No unverified price, guarantee or urgent-damage language. Check current account defaults, network expansion and generated-asset settings; keep the first test within the approved scope. Review actual search terms for irrelevant DIY parts, jobs/careers, grading and other unoffered work before adding negatives; do not exclude legitimate repair or price-comparison callers automatically.

**Spend control:** Google's average daily budget is not a hard daily cap; for most campaigns daily spending can reach twice that average and the usual monthly limit is 30.4 times it. A short run is not automatically capped at its daily average times its length. Confirm the applicable budget type, pacing and stop controls before approval; do not promise an exact unattended cap. [Google spending limits](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10486637?hl=en)

**Phase 2: review actual calls and jobs.** Pause if contact routing fails, capacity is unavailable, spend approaches the agreed stop threshold, or out-of-scope calls dominate. If fit is good but bookings lag, inspect scope, availability and follow-up before increasing reach. Continue only when attributable suitable bookings and economics support it; clicks alone never justify scaling.

**Phase 3: a second channel, separately approved.** Check LSA category/location eligibility first if relevant. Otherwise choose one Meta or Nextdoor lesson/call test, with the same qualification definitions and its own approved cap. Avoid retargeting, uploaded customer lists, automated outreach and multi-channel expansion until there is a clear need and separate authorization.
